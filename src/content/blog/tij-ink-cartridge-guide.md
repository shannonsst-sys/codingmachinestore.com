Every TIJ printer in our range runs on the same consumable: a 42 ml thermal inkjet (TIJ) cartridge. It is a small plastic block with the print head built in — when the ink runs out, you swap the whole cartridge, and the printer is as good as new. No cleaning, no nozzles to unblock, no service call. That simplicity is the point of TIJ. But there are two sizes, three ink types and two price bands, and buying the wrong one is the most common beginner mistake. This guide explains what each choice means.

## 12.7 mm vs 25.4 mm: it is about print height, not quality

The number is the width of the print head — and therefore the maximum height of one printed line.

- **12.7 mm cartridges** print up to 12.7 mm high in a single pass. That covers a normal date code, batch number, or one line of text, which is what most buyers need. They fit every standard TIJ printer we sell, from the [M70 mini](/products/m70-mini-inkjet-printer/) to the [A5 handheld](/products/a5-handheld-inkjet-printer/) and the [T6 online printer](/products/t6-online-inkjet-printer/).
- **25.4 mm cartridges** print up to 25.4 mm high — twice as tall. They are for large dates, big batch codes, logos, or two lines of text printed in one pass. They fit the "S" versions of our printers: A3S, A5S, A9S, A5CS, T5S, T6S, T9S and T10S-1.

There is a third way to reach 25.4 mm: two heads. The [P500 two-head handheld](/products/p500-handheld-inkjet-printer/) and the [T10-2 two-head online printer](/products/t10-2-online-inkjet-printer/) stack two 12.7 mm cartridges side by side to print 25.4 mm high. Each head takes its own cartridge, so a two-head machine uses two cartridges — one per head.

**Which size do you need?** Measure the tallest mark you print. If it fits in 12.7 mm — a date plus a batch code on one line, for example — the 12.7 mm cartridge is the right one, and it is the cheaper one. Only buy 25.4 mm if your message is genuinely taller, or if your printer is an S model that takes it. A 25.4 mm cartridge does not fit a standard 12.7 mm printer, so check the model letter before you order. ## Water-based, solvent or oil-based: match the ink to the surface

The ink type matters more than the cartridge size. The wrong ink will not dry, will smudge, or will wipe straight off. The rule is simple:

- **Water-based ink** is for porous surfaces: paper, cardboard cartons, wood, uncoated labels. It soaks in and stays. It is the cheapest option and the right one for most carton coding.
- **Solvent quick-dry ink** is for non-porous surfaces: plastic film, PET and HDPE bottles, glass, metal, foil and coated cartons. It dries in seconds on surfaces water-based ink cannot grip. If you print on plastic or glass, this is the ink you need.
- **Oil-based ink** sits between the two and suits some coated papers and semi-porous surfaces.

Our cartridges come in black, white, red, blue, yellow and green. Black is the default for dates and batch codes; white ink is for dark surfaces where black would disappear — dark glass bottles, black plastic, coloured cartons.

If you are unsure which ink your surface needs, test it: print your real message on your real packaging, let it dry for a few seconds, then rub it with a dry thumb and a damp cloth. If it smudges or wipes off, you have the wrong ink. That five-minute test is cheaper than a pallet of smeared cartons.

## How long does one cartridge last?

There is no honest single number, and any supplier who gives you one without asking what you print is guessing. A 42 ml cartridge holds a fixed amount of ink, so how many prints you get depends entirely on how much ink each print uses:

- A short date code ("EXP 12/2027") uses very little ink — thousands of prints per cartridge.
- A long message with a logo, a barcode and a QR code uses many times more ink per print.
- Larger characters, bold fonts and higher print density all use more ink.
- A 25.4 mm cartridge prints a taller mark, so the same message uses more ink per print than on a 12.7 mm cartridge — but the cartridge holds the same 42 ml.

The only number that matters is your own. Print your real message on your real packaging, count how many prints one cartridge gives you, and divide the cartridge price by that number. That is your cost per print. Our cost guide, [how much does an inkjet printer cost](/blog/how-much-does-an-inkjet-printer-cost/), works through the maths with a real example.

Two things that do shorten cartridge life: printing with the head too far from the surface (the printer compensates by firing more ink), and leaving a cartridge uncapped. Our printers print best at a distance of 2–5 mm from the surface — keep the head close and you use less ink and get a sharper print.

## Storing and handling cartridges

A TIJ cartridge is a sealed unit with the print head built in, so there is almost nothing to maintain — but a little care goes a long way:

1. **Keep the nozzle capped when the printer is not in use.** The clip-on cap stops the ink drying on the nozzles. A dried nozzle prints with missing lines; a capped one is ready to print.
2. **Do not touch the nozzle plate.** Fingerprints and dust block nozzles. Handle the cartridge by its body.
3. **Store cartridges upright, in a cool place, out of direct sunlight.** Heat thickens ink and shortens shelf life.
4. **Use the cartridge in the printer it was bought for.** A 12.7 mm cartridge will not work in a 25.4 mm printer, and vice versa — the printer will not recognise it.
5. **When print quality drops, swap the cartridge first.** Faded or patchy printing on a TIJ printer almost always means the cartridge is empty or a nozzle is blocked, not that the printer is broken. A fresh cartridge is the cheapest diagnostic there is.

## Which cartridge should you order?

- **Printing dates and batch codes on cartons or paper?** A 12.7 mm cartridge with water-based ink, from $30.
- **Printing on plastic, glass, metal or coated cartons?** A 12.7 mm cartridge with solvent quick-dry ink, from $30.
- **Need a tall mark, a logo or two lines in one pass?** A 25.4 mm cartridge for an S-model printer, from $50 — or a two-head printer that uses two 12.7 mm cartridges.
- **Printing on dark surfaces?** Ask for white ink.

Order at least one spare cartridge with your printer. A printer with no ink is a paperweight, and a spare cartridge is the cheapest insurance against a stopped line.
