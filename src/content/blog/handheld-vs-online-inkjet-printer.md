A buyer choosing their first inkjet coder usually asks the same question: should I buy a handheld printer I hold in my hand, or an online printer that sits over a conveyor? Both are thermal inkjet (TIJ) printers, both use the same cartridges, and both print dates, batch codes, barcodes and QR codes. The difference is how the product moves past the printhead — and that decides everything else.

## What is a handheld inkjet printer?

A handheld inkjet printer is a pistol-grip machine with a touchscreen, a battery and a cartridge in the head. The operator picks it up, presses the head against the product and pulls the trigger. The print happens as the operator moves the printer across the surface.

Handheld printers suit work that a conveyor cannot do: cartons already stacked on a pallet, large boxes, pipes, steel plates, wooden boards, and products too big or too heavy to run down a line. The operator walks to the product instead of the product coming to the machine.

Typical handheld models have a 4.3-inch or 5-inch touchscreen, print 12.7 mm high with one cartridge (25.4 mm with two heads), and run on a rechargeable battery. A [mini handheld printer](/products/m70-mini-inkjet-printer/) fits in one hand for small cartons; a [two-head handheld](/products/p500-handheld-inkjet-printer/) prints 25.4 mm high in a single pass for large dates and logos. Multi-head models such as the [T-4](/products/t-4-handheld-inkjet-printer/) print up to 100 mm high.

## What is an online inkjet printer?

An online inkjet printer is fixed in place — usually on a bracket above a conveyor or a paging machine. A sensor or synchronizer wheel detects each product as it passes and triggers the print at exactly the same position every time. The operator does not touch the printer during production.

Online printers suit volume. Once set up, they code hundreds or thousands of products an hour with consistent placement and no labour per product. A single-head [online printer](/products/t6-online-inkjet-printer/) codes cartons on a line; a [two-head online printer](/products/t10-2-online-inkjet-printer/) covers 25.4 mm; a [conveyor printer](/products/k80-conveyor-inkjet-printer/) arrives with its own belt so it works as a standalone coding station.

## Handheld vs online: the comparison

|  | Handheld inkjet printer | Online inkjet printer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How it works | Operator holds it and moves it across the product | Fixed head; conveyor moves the product |
| Throughput | Tens to hundreds of marks per hour, operator-dependent | Hundreds to thousands per hour, line-dependent |
| Print placement | Depends on the operator's hand | Same position on every product, sensor-triggered |
| Flexibility | Codes anything, anywhere — pallets, pipes, large items | Codes only what passes the line |
| Labour | One operator per printer while coding | Set up once, then runs unattended |
| Print height | 12.7 mm per head, up to 100 mm with four heads | 12.7 mm per head, up to 25.4 mm with two heads |
| Setup | Unbox, load a cartridge, print | Bracket, sensor, conveyor alignment |
| Cost | Lower upfront cost, easy to start | Higher upfront cost, lower cost per mark at volume |

## Which one should you choose?

**Choose a handheld printer if:**

- You code cartons after packing, on pallets, or in a warehouse with no conveyor.
- Your products are large, heavy, or irregularly shaped.
- Your volume is low to medium, or your products and messages change every job.
- Different people need to code in different places.
- You want the lowest upfront cost and the fastest start.

**Choose an online printer if:**

- You already have a conveyor, a filling line or a packaging line.
- You code hundreds of products an hour and need the same position on every one.
- You want coding to happen with no operator standing at the line.
- Your products are small and uniform — bottles, small cartons, sachets, labels.
- You are automating and want the coder to run with the line.

**Many buyers end up with both.** A handheld printer codes pallets and rework, while an online printer handles the main line. Because both use the same 12.7 mm and 25.4 mm cartridges, one cartridge stock serves the whole workshop — and every inkjet printer we list includes one cartridge per print head.

## What to check before you buy

1. **Print height.** Measure the tallest mark you need. One head covers 12.7 mm; two heads cover 25.4 mm; four heads cover 100 mm. Buy the height you need, not the height you might need.
2. **Surface and ink.** Porous cartons take water-based ink. Coated boxes, plastic and metal need solvent quick-dry or oil-based ink. Tell the supplier your surface and they will match the cartridge.
3. **Message content.** Dates and batch codes fit any printer. Barcodes and QR codes need the 300–600 dpi resolution that TIJ gives — check the print sample, not just the specification sheet.
4. **Line speed and trigger.** Online printers need a sensor or synchronizer to fire at the right moment. If your line speed varies, a synchronizer wheel keeps the print length constant.
5. **Running cost.** Work out the cost per mark from the cartridge price. A 12.7 mm cartridge costs from $30 — divide that by the number of prints per cartridge and you have your real running cost.
6. **Who operates it.** A touchscreen with your language and simple message editing saves more time than any specification.

## The short version

If the product comes to you, buy online. If you go to the product, buy handheld. If you are not sure yet, start with a handheld printer — it is the cheapest way to start coding, and the cartridges and know-how transfer directly to an online printer later.
