Ask "how much does an inkjet printer cost?" and the honest answer is: it depends on what you count. The machine is only the first payment. The cartridge is the cost that follows you every day, and it is the number that decides whether a cheap printer is actually cheap.

## The machine: $180 to $880

A handheld thermal inkjet (TIJ) printer is the cheapest way into date coding. Our handheld range starts at the M70 mini at $180, the T-Z at $187 and the A3 at $193, with the A5 at $210 and the A9 at $255 for larger screens and more features. These are standalone printers: you hold them against the product and pull the trigger.

Online and conveyor printers, which mount over a belt and print as products pass underneath, start at the T6 at $230 and the T9 at $243. Desktop models such as the K90 sit at $250. All of these are single-head machines printing one line up to 12.7 mm high.

Multi-head printers cost more because they print taller marks — and because each head needs its own cartridge. The P500 two-head handheld is $337, the T10-2 two-head online printer is $425, and the T-4 four-head handheld is $880, printing up to 100 mm high. Large-format and feeding models such as the F-11 sit at $605. Some specialist models are priced on enquiry — if a machine shows no price, it means we could not verify a reliable price, not that it is free.

Every inkjet printer we sell includes one cartridge per print head, so it prints from day one. There is no minimum order quantity, so you can start with a single machine. Shipping is quoted separately.

## The cartridge: $30 to $54, and it is the real cost

TIJ printers use 42 ml [cartridges](/products/tij-cartridge-12-7mm/) in two widths: 12.7 mm and 25.4 mm. A 12.7 mm cartridge costs from $30 for a domestic cartridge and $50 for an imported one; a 25.4 mm cartridge costs from $50 for a domestic cartridge and $54 for an imported one. The ink type — water-based, solvent quick-dry or oil-based — matches your surface, not your budget: porous paper takes water-based ink, while plastic film, foil and coated cartons need solvent quick-dry.

How long does one cartridge last? That is the question that decides your running cost, and there is no single honest number. It depends on how much ink each print uses: a short date code uses far less ink than a long message with a logo and a QR code. As a rule of thumb, the fewer characters and the smaller the print, the more prints you get per cartridge — but the only number that matters is your own. Print your real message on your real packaging, count how many prints one cartridge gives you, and divide the cartridge price by that number. That is your cost per print.

## The hidden costs buyers forget

**Brackets and sensors.** An online printer needs a mounting bracket over your conveyor and a sensor to trigger each print. Budget for the bracket and check whether your line already has a sensor position — a printer that cannot be mounted cleanly is a printer that prints in the wrong place.

**Shipping.** Our prices exclude shipping, which is quoted separately based on where you are and how many machines you order. For a single handheld printer this is a small part of the total; for a heavy conveyor setup it matters more. Ask for the shipping quote before you compare prices across suppliers.

**Spare cartridges.** Order at least one spare cartridge with the machine. A printer with no ink is a paperweight, and a spare cartridge is the cheapest insurance against a stopped line.

**Your time.** A printer that takes ten minutes to change a message costs you ten minutes every changeover. TIJ printers edit messages on a touchscreen, so a date change takes seconds.

## How inkjet compares with the alternatives

If your only job is fixed text on paper — a date and a batch code on a paper bag or carton, no barcode — an ink roller coder is cheaper to run. A solid ink roll costs $0.50, and the machines start at $194.05 for the LS-1100 and $232.90 for the HP-280. But ink rollers print fixed text with type characters only: no barcodes, no QR codes, no variable data.

A hot ribbon coder prints on flexible film and foil with a ribbon that costs $0.80 a roll, and the machines start at $47.28 for the DY-8B. Again, text only — and the ribbon is a consumable you replace regularly.

Inkjet costs more per print than either of those, but it is the only one of the three that prints variable data, barcodes and QR codes — and it prints on almost any surface with the right ink. If you need a scannable code, inkjet is not the expensive option: it is the only option.

## A worked example

Say you code 500 cartons a day with a short date and batch code, five days a week, using an [A5 handheld](/products/a5-handheld-inkjet-printer/) at $210 and a 12.7 mm cartridge at $30. Your machine cost is $210 once. Suppose one cartridge codes 5,000 prints of your message. That is $0.006 per carton, or $3 a day in ink. Over a year, the ink costs far more than the machine, which is exactly why the cartridge price — not the machine price — is the number to compare when you choose between two printers.

## What to ask before you buy

1. **What does the price include?** Ours includes one cartridge per print head. Check whether a cheaper quote elsewhere includes a cartridge at all.
2. **How much is a replacement cartridge, and how many prints does it give for my message?** That is your running cost.
3. **Does the printer fit my line?** Check the print height, the mounting, and whether you need a sensor.
4. **What ink does my surface need?** Water-based for paper, solvent quick-dry for plastic and coated board — the wrong ink is the most common expensive mistake.
5. **What is the shipping cost, and is there a minimum order?** We have no minimum order, and shipping is quoted separately.
