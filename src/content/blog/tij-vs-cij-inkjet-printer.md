If you need to print dates, batch numbers, barcodes or QR codes on packaging, you will meet two technologies first: TIJ (thermal inkjet) and CIJ (continuous inkjet). Both are industrial inkjet printers, but they work in completely different ways — and the wrong choice costs you money every day in ink, maintenance or downtime.

This guide explains how each one works, what it costs to run, and which one fits your products and your line speed.

## What is a TIJ printer?

TIJ stands for thermal inkjet. Inside the printhead, tiny heaters create a vapour bubble that pushes a drop of ink out of the nozzle and onto the product. The key point: **the ink cartridge is the printhead**. When the cartridge runs out, you swap in a fresh one and you have a brand-new printhead — no cleaning, no service visit.

Modern TIJ printers use the HP TIJ 2.5 platform. One 42 ml cartridge prints up to 12.7 mm high; two cartridges side by side print 25.4 mm. Resolution runs from 300 to 600 dpi, which is sharp enough for small text, logos, barcodes and QR codes that a phone or scanner can read. The print distance is short, about 2–5 mm, so the head sits close to the product.

TIJ inks come in water-based, solvent quick-dry and oil-based versions, so the same printer can mark cartons, coated boxes, plastic bottles, bags, metal and wood. Messages can include text, dates, expiry dates, batch and lot codes, counters, logos, barcodes and QR codes — all set from a touchscreen.

## What is a CIJ printer?

CIJ stands for continuous inkjet. Instead of firing drops on demand, a CIJ printer pushes a continuous stream of ink through a nozzle, breaks it into droplets, and charges selected droplets so they are deflected onto the product. The uncharged drops are collected and reused.

CIJ is the classic "small character" printer for high-speed lines. It prints small text at very high line speeds, on almost any surface — including curved glass, metal, cable and plastic — because the ink is thrown across a gap of several millimetres. It uses solvent-based inks that dry fast on non-porous surfaces.

The trade-off is maintenance. A CIJ printer has an ink system with filters, pumps and solvent, and it needs regular cleaning, solvent top-ups and filter changes. If a line stops for a weekend, the printer usually needs a cleaning cycle before it runs cleanly again.

## TIJ vs CIJ: the comparison

|  | TIJ (thermal inkjet) | CIJ (continuous inkjet) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Print height | 12.7 mm per head, up to 100 mm with multi-head models | Typically 1–2 lines, up to about 10–15 mm |
| Resolution | 300–600 dpi, sharp barcodes and QR codes | Lower resolution, best for small text |
| Line speed | Suits low to medium speeds; handheld and online models | Very high speeds on fast production lines |
| Surfaces | Cartons, coated boxes, bottles, bags, metal, wood | Almost any surface, including curved and non-porous |
| Maintenance | Almost none — replace the cartridge, get a new printhead | Regular cleaning, filters, solvent, service |
| Running cost | Ink cartridges; 12.7 mm cartridges from $30 | Ink + solvent + filters + service visits |
| Upfront cost | Low — handheld TIJ printers are the cheapest way into coding | Higher — industrial CIJ is a bigger investment |
| Best for | Variable data, barcodes, QR codes, small to medium runs | High-speed lines that never stop |

## What does each one cost to run?

With TIJ, the running cost is simple: the cartridge. A 12.7 mm cartridge costs from $30 (domestic ink) and a 25.4 mm cartridge from $50, and one cartridge prints a known number of marks — you can work out the cost per carton before you buy. There is no solvent to top up and no filter to change.

With CIJ, the cost is ink plus solvent plus filters plus the service time to keep the ink system clean. On a fast line that runs all day, that cost is spread over a huge number of products, so the cost per mark can be very low. On a line that runs a few hours a day, the fixed maintenance cost weighs much heavier.

## Which one should you choose?

**Choose TIJ if:**

- You print variable data — dates, batch codes, QR codes, barcodes that change every job.
- Your line runs at low to medium speed, or you code by hand, in a warehouse, or on pallets.
- You want clean, simple operation with no solvent smell and no daily cleaning.
- You print on cartons, boxes, bottles and bags and want sharp, scannable codes.
- Your budget is limited and you want to start coding this week.

**Choose CIJ if:**

- Your line runs fast — hundreds of products a minute — and never stops.
- You print only small text (date and lot code) on difficult surfaces like glass or cable.
- You already have staff who know how to maintain an ink system.
- The cost of a stopped line is higher than the cost of maintaining the printer.
