If you have ever bought a date coding machine, you have probably seen the letters TIJ. It stands for thermal inkjet, and it is the printing technology inside most of the handheld and online inkjet coders on the market today — including every inkjet printer we sell. This guide explains, in plain English, what a thermal inkjet printer is, how it works, what it can and cannot print on, and how to tell whether it is the right technology for your packaging.

## How TIJ printing works

A thermal inkjet printer works a lot like the inkjet printer on an office desk, but built for a factory floor or a packing table instead of paper.

Inside every TIJ cartridge there is a tiny print head with hundreds of microscopic nozzles. Each nozzle sits above a small heating element. When the printer needs a dot of ink, it heats that element for a fraction of a second. The heat makes a tiny bubble in the ink, and the bubble pushes a droplet out of the nozzle and onto your product. Thousands of these droplets, fired in the right pattern, form letters, numbers, barcodes, QR codes and logos.

The key detail: **the print head is built into the cartridge, not the printer.** When the ink runs out, you throw the cartridge away and clip in a new one — and with it comes a brand-new print head. There are no nozzles to clean, no print heads to unblock, and no service engineer to call. That is the whole appeal of TIJ: the part that wears out is the part you replace anyway.

Most TIJ printers on the market today, including ours, are built on the HP TIJ 2.5 platform — the same cartridge standard used across the industry. That is why our [12.7 mm cartridges](/products/tij-cartridge-12-7mm/) fit every standard TIJ printer we sell, and why a cartridge costs from $30 instead of hundreds.

## What a TIJ printer can print

A TIJ printer prints variable data — information that changes from one product to the next:

- **Dates:** production dates, expiry dates, best-before dates
- **Batch and lot codes:** for traceability and recalls
- **Serial numbers and counters:** sequential numbering, one product at a time
- **Barcodes and QR codes:** scannable codes for retail, logistics and anti-counterfeit checks
- **Text and logos:** product names, brand marks, warnings, multi-line messages

One print head prints up to 12.7 mm high in a single pass — enough for a date code and a batch number on one line. Printers that take a [25.4 mm cartridge](/products/tij-cartridge-25-4mm/), or that stack two 12.7 mm heads side by side, print up to 25.4 mm high, which covers taller text, two lines at once, or a small logo. For even taller marks, multi-head machines such as the [T-4 four-head handheld](/products/t-4-handheld-inkjet-printer/) print up to 100 mm high.

Print quality is sharp on the right surface: clean, dark characters that look printed, not stamped. Because the droplets are so small, TIJ handles small text and fine QR codes better than most other coding technologies — which is why it is the default choice when a barcode has to scan at a checkout or a warehouse.

## What surfaces does TIJ print on?

TIJ prints on almost any flat or slightly curved surface, as long as you match the ink to the material. The cartridge size sets the print height; the ink type sets which surfaces it works on:

- **Water-based ink** is for porous surfaces: paper, cardboard cartons, wood and uncoated labels. It soaks in and stays. It is the cheapest ink and the right choice for most carton coding.
- **Solvent quick-dry ink** is for non-porous surfaces: plastic film, PET and HDPE bottles, glass, metal, foil and coated cartons. It dries in seconds on surfaces water-based ink cannot grip.
- **Oil-based ink** sits between the two and suits some coated papers and semi-porous surfaces.

Cartridges come in black, white, red, blue, yellow and green. Black is the default for dates and batch codes; white ink is for dark surfaces where black would disappear.

For best results, the print head should sit 2–5 mm from the surface. Too far away and the print goes fuzzy and uses more ink; too close and the cartridge can catch on the product. ## Handheld, online or desktop: which TIJ printer?

TIJ is the technology; the printer is the machine around it. There are three shapes, and the right one depends on how your products move:

- **Handheld TIJ printers** are held in the hand and pressed against the product — for small batches, irregular items, or products that are too big to move. The [M70 mini handheld](/products/m70-mini-inkjet-printer/) is the smallest and cheapest at $180, while the [A5 handheld](/products/a5-handheld-inkjet-printer/) at $210 adds a metal shell and a bigger screen. See our comparison of [handheld vs online inkjet printers](/blog/handheld-vs-online-inkjet-printer/) for the full trade-off.
- **Online TIJ printers** are bolted above a conveyor and print as products pass underneath — for production lines that run continuously. The [T6 online printer](/products/t6-online-inkjet-printer/) at $230 is a typical starting point, and multi-head models print taller marks in one pass.
- **Desktop TIJ printers** sit on a bench and print on items fed through by hand or by a small conveyor — for workshops that code the same product all day in one place. The [K90 desktop printer](/products/k90-desktop-inkjet-printer/) at $250 is built for that job.

All three use the same cartridges and the same ink, so the printing itself is identical — only the way the product moves past the head changes.

## What does a TIJ printer cost to run?

The machine is the small part of the bill. A handheld TIJ printer starts at $180 and an online one at $220, and every printer we sell includes one cartridge per print head, so you start printing on day one. The running cost is the cartridge: from $30 for a domestic 12.7 mm cartridge and $50 for an imported one, with 25.4 mm cartridges from $50. How many prints you get per cartridge depends on what you print — a short date code uses very little ink, a logo and a QR code use much more. Our guide on [how much an inkjet printer costs](/blog/how-much-does-an-inkjet-printer-cost/) works through the maths with a real example.

Maintenance costs are close to zero. There is no print head to replace, no pump, no filter and no solvent to top up. The only regular job is keeping the cartridge capped when the printer is not in use, so the ink does not dry on the nozzles.

## When TIJ is not the right choice

TIJ is the most versatile coding technology, but it is not always the cheapest or the best fit:

- **Printing only on plastic bags or film, in high volume?** A hot ribbon coder such as the [HP-241B](/products/hp-241b/) prints directly on film for a fraction of a cent per print, and a ribbon costs $0.80 a roll. See [hot ribbon coder vs inkjet printer](/blog/hot-ribbon-coder-vs-inkjet-printer/) for the cost comparison.
- **Printing on paper bags or labels with simple text?** An ink roller coder such as the [HP-280](/products/hp-280/) is simpler and cheaper to run.
- **Need to print on very rough, wet or oily surfaces?** Ask us first — some surfaces defeat every ink, and a quick test on your real packaging is worth more than any datasheet.
