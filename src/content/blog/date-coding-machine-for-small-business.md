If you run a small business — a food workshop, a cosmetics maker, a small e-commerce seller — sooner or later a customer, a retailer or a regulator asks for a date on your product. A production date, an expiry date, a batch code. And the question that follows is always the same: what is the cheapest machine that does the job properly?

The good news: you do not need a production line or a big budget to start date coding. You can start for under $50. The trick is matching the machine to what you actually print on, how many you print, and how the print needs to look. Buy too cheap and the print smudges or the machine cannot handle your packaging; buy too much machine and you have paid for capacity you will never use.

## Step 1: what are you printing on?

The surface decides the technology, and the technology decides the price.

- **Plastic bags, pouches or film?** A hot ribbon coder is the cheapest route. It melts ink from a ribbon onto the film with a heated type block. The print is crisp, it does not smudge, and a roll of ribbon costs $0.80.
- **Paper bags, cartons or labels?** Either a ribbon coder or an ink roller coder works, and both are cheap. Ink roller machines are the simplest of all — a heated ink roll and movable type, no electronics at all.
- **Bottles, jars, boxes or anything curved or hard?** You need an inkjet printer. Ribbon and ink roller machines need a flat, soft surface to press against; a handheld inkjet printer just needs to be held 2–5 mm from the surface.
- **A mix of everything?** A handheld inkjet printer is the most flexible single machine, because the same printer with the right ink handles cartons, plastic, glass and metal.

If you are not sure which surface group your packaging falls into, print this test in your head: can you press a rubber stamp flat against it? If yes, a ribbon or ink roller coder works. If no — it is round, hard or uneven — you need inkjet.

## What about second-hand or the very cheapest printer online?

A word of caution. The cheapest listing on a marketplace is often a machine with no support, no spare parts and no manual in English. A date coding machine is a tool you will use every day; when it stops, your packing stops. Buying from a supplier that stocks the consumables — ribbons, type, cartridges — and answers questions is worth more than saving $20 on the machine. That is also why we list every consumable with its price: a $47 machine is only cheap if the $0.80 ribbon is actually available when you need it.

## Three things to do before you order

1. **Test on your real packaging.** Print your actual date on your actual bag, bottle or carton, then rub it with a dry thumb and a damp cloth. If it smudges, you need a different ink or a different technology — better to find out before you order than after.
2. **Count your daily volume honestly.** Under 200 prints a day, a manual machine is fine. Over that, pay for the electric or handheld version; your time is worth more than the price difference.
3. **Order the consumables with the machine.** A ribbon coder without ribbon, or an inkjet printer without a spare cartridge, is a paperweight on your busiest day. A spare ribbon costs $0.80 and a spare cartridge from $30 — the cheapest insurance you will ever buy.
